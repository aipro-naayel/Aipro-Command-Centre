University project · proposal and research log

AI-assisted carpooling for university communities.

A proposed web and mobile experience for making shared journeys easier to discover, coordinate, and evaluate. The project explores how responsible AI can support ride matching without turning personal mobility data into a black box.

Design proposalPeer review readyFuture work documented

Project brief

A clear problem statement keeps the technology in service of people, not the other way around.

University travel often involves repeated routes, changing timetables, and limited information about who is willing to share a journey. This project proposes a carpooling platform where verified community members can offer or request seats and receive explainable match suggestions.

The AI component is deliberately narrow: rank compatible journey options using stated preferences, route overlap, time windows, and available capacity. The system should assist a user's decision, while leaving acceptance, contact, and travel arrangements under human control.

This is a proposed architecture and evaluation plan. Claims about performance, safety, adoption, carbon reduction, or cost should only be made after data collection and testing.

Useful before clever

A strong baseline and a simple interface come before complex models.

Consent by design

Collect only what is needed, explain why it is needed, and provide controls for visibility.

Human decision-making

Recommendations support a choice; they never silently make a travel or safety decision.

System schema

A high-level data and service flow for a first research prototype.

Proposed architecture
Community interfaceOffer a seat, request a ride, set preferences, review a suggestion.
→
Journey serviceValidate input, manage consent, store minimal journey and availability records.
→
Matching layerFilter constraints, rank compatible options, return reasons and uncertainty.

Supporting services: authentication, notifications, moderation, audit logging, and an evaluation dataset with identifiers removed or controlled.

Input

Stated constraints

Origin area, destination area, time window, seats, accessibility needs, and ride preferences. Exact addresses should be avoided until necessary.

Decision

Explainable ranking

Return match factors such as route overlap, time fit, and available capacity, rather than presenting an unexplained score.

Output

Human confirmation

Show a shortlist, let people compare options, and require explicit consent before sharing contact or trip details.

Development workflow

A staged route from evidence to prototype to reviewable results.

01 · Discover

Map the need

Interview intended users, identify journeys, risks, and access needs.

02 · Define

Set the baseline

Write matching rules, success measures, consent flows, and out-of-scope cases.

03 · Prototype

Build narrowly

Create the interface and a transparent rule-based matcher before testing AI variants.

04 · Evaluate

Test the trade-offs

Compare quality, fairness, privacy, usability, latency, and failure cases.

05 · Report

Make it reviewable

Publish methods, limitations, sample definitions, and what remains unproven.

Peer review plan

Evidence that can support a credible university submission rather than a polished demo alone.

Matching qualityRelevance

Do users find the suggested rides compatible with their stated constraints?

ExperienceTask success

Can a participant offer or request a journey without confusion or avoidable friction?

ResponsibilityData minimisation

Is each collected field justified, protected, and removable by the user?

FairnessCoverage

Do suggestions work across different schedules, locations, and access needs?

OperationsReliability

How does the service behave with missing data, no match, or stale availability?

TrustExplainability

Can a participant understand why an option appeared and challenge it?

Questions for reviewers

Invite criticism around the system's assumptions, not only its visual finish.

  • Is AI necessary for the proposed task?
  • Are the inputs proportionate to the benefit?
  • What is the safest no-match experience?
  • Which users are underrepresented?
  • What evidence would change the design?

Future enhancement workflow

Potential directions after the core prototype has evidence, consent, and a measurable baseline.

Next

Better availability

Explore calendar integrations only with explicit permission, clear revocation, and a strong reason to use them.

Later

Personalised ranking

Test whether preference-aware ranking improves usefulness without creating unfair exclusion or opaque decisions.

Research

Privacy-preserving learning

Investigate aggregation or on-device approaches if the project eventually needs learning from repeated interactions.

Community

Trust and moderation

Design reporting, verification, reputation, and escalation with user research rather than assuming ratings solve safety.

Sustainability

Impact measurement

Measure occupancy and avoided journeys carefully; do not claim environmental benefit without a defined comparison.

Scale

Service resilience

Plan for stale data, outages, abuse, and changing university policies before expanding beyond a pilot.

A credible project is allowed to have limits.

The ambition is to make shared university travel easier to explore while making every assumption visible. The next step is not to promise an intelligent city; it is to test one narrow journey workflow with care.
